# Rules behind the banner

The legal sources that set what a cookie banner must do, and how Consent Kit meets each one. Every link goes to the official source and was checked on 7 November 2026. This is a reference for developers and site owners, not legal advice.

## United States (California)

There is no federal cookie-consent law. California's model is opt-out, and it binds only businesses over its thresholds.

| Source | Provision | What it means for the banner | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| CCPA as amended by CPRA, Civil Code | Section 1798.140(d) | Applies to for-profit businesses in California with revenue over $26,625,000 (from 1 January 2025), or that buy, sell or share data on 100,000 or more consumers or households a year, or that earn half their revenue from selling or sharing it. | [Section 1798.140](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.140), [CPPA thresholds](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/cpi_adjustment.html) |
| CCPA | Sections 1798.120 and 1798.135 | Consumers can opt out of the sale or sharing of their data, through a "Do Not Sell or Share" link or by the business honouring opt-out preference signals. | [Section 1798.120](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.120), [Section 1798.135](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.135) |
| CCPA Regulations, effective 1 January 2026 | 11 CCR section 7025 | A valid opt-out preference signal, such as Global Privacy Control, must be treated as an opt-out for that browser. | [CCPA Regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/ccpa_statute_eff_20260101.pdf), [Attorney General CCPA page](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa) |

|---|---|---|

## How Consent Kit meets these

- **Nothing optional before a choice.** Analytics and any host in `wp.block` (or any script marked `type="text/plain" data-consent="granted"`) wait for Accept. This covers ePrivacy Article 5(3), LSSI article 22.2 and PECR regulation 6.
- **Reject as easy as Accept.** Both buttons are on the first layer, side by side, the same size and style. This covers AEPD 3.2.3 and the EDPB guidelines.
- **Change your mind at any time.** Any link to `#cookie-settings` reopens the banner. Put one in every page footer. This covers GDPR and UK GDPR Article 7(3) and AEPD 3.2.9.
- **Proof of consent.** Each choice is logged with a random ID (also kept in the visitor's cookie), the choice, the policy version, the page and the time, with no IP address (GDPR Article 7(1), EDPB paragraphs 106-107). The WordPress plugin also keeps what the banner said for each policy version, so a record can be matched to the wording the visitor saw (EDPB paragraph 108). Sites that don't use WordPress should keep their banner text in version control for the same reason.
- **Asking again.** Raising `version` asks every visitor again, as AEPD 3.2.7 and EDPB paragraph 110 require when purposes or third parties change. A choice lasts 180 days by default, well inside the AEPD's 24 months.
- **Global Privacy Control.** With `respectGpc` on (the default), a browser sending GPC is treated as Reject until the visitor chooses otherwise, which meets 11 CCR section 7025 where the CCPA applies. GPC is never read as Accept.
- **Google Consent Mode.** The default is set to denied before any Google tag. For sites with EU or Spanish visitors, prefer basic mode, where Google loads only after Accept. Advanced mode sends cookieless pings before a choice, which the EDPB's 2/2023 guidelines treat as needing consent. On WordPress, adding `googletagmanager.com` to `wp.block` gives basic mode. In React, render the Google tag inside `<ConsentGate>`.
- **What the policy page must say.** Planet49 and the Google policy require naming each cookie, how long it lasts, and which third parties receive data. Consent Kit doesn't write the policy, so keep the policy page in step with the banner.
